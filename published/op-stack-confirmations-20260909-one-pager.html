OP Stack / Engineering proposalSeptember 2026

Transaction latency falls between team responsibilities.

Existing monitoring covers individual components. No one owns joining them into a continuous view of transaction latency from first receipt to usable confirmation. Engineering lacks a common baseline for choosing optimizations; Data lacks the linked history needed to explain sub-block behavior over time.

Proposed decision

Assign one engineering DRI to Stage 1, partnered with Data. Use the baseline to scope notifications and set latency targets.

01Measure the transaction lifecycle

Problem. Our best OP Sepolia configuration achieved ~60 ms median and 67 ms p99 across 1,000 simple transfers, with submissions timed to sub-block production. That measured client submission to first sub-block receipt, including network latency. It cannot tell us where time was spent inside the OP Stack.

Solution. Verify existing monitoring in production and connect the ingress, builder and RPC code paths. Record first receipt, builder execution, sub-block publication and serving-RPC result availability. Export Prometheus histograms for receipt-to-publication and receipt-to-result latency, with coverage and unmatched transactions visible. Correlate events by transaction and payload identity, accounting for clock skew and abandoned builds. Existing histogram ↗

Data engineering also needs retained timestamps and exact transaction-to-sub-block membership for months of builder-policy analysis. Histograms discard those identities; reconstructing membership from sealed blocks is unreliable. Agree the event schema, coverage and retention with Data. Onchain encoding is a separate design decision.

Stage 1 is complete when we have a verified, continuous latency baseline with known coverage, and retained records that Data can join to sub-blocks and canonical blocks.

02Make confirmations useful without polling

Problem. Applications reconcile RPC acknowledgments, sub-block streams and execution results themselves. A returned transaction hash does not establish ordering.

Solution. Build on existing streams and RPCs to support subscriptions to ordering confirmation, execution confirmation, or both. Provide receipt status and logs, with resulting state accessible through RPC. Define behavior for reverts, transactions without logs, revocations and reconnects. The signals may arrive together and remain provisional. Sub-block semantics ↗

03Reduce measured waiting and variance

Problem. A fast sub-block cadence can still leave transactions waiting on transport, buffering or builder policy.

Solution. Test low-hanging fruit: eliminate mempool P2P from the path to the builder where delivery still depends on it, using direct routes; remove redundant handoffs and timer-driven waits. Distinguish transport delay from gas-capacity and policy deferrals. Compare median, p95/p99 and variance under matched workloads, traffic and submission timing, retaining misses and slow transactions.

Measurement definitions: builder execution precedes sub-block publication; getPayload is not a pre-execution milestone. Result availability is measured at the serving RPC. Capture actual event times; block timestamps and sub-block indices do not measure elapsed latency.

Benchmark: OP Sepolia, 8 September 2026; 59.7 ms median, 67.1 ms p99. Signing, connection preparation and deliberate pre-submission waiting were outside the clock. The result describes this testnet workload and timing strategy.
