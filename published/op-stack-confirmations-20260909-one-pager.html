OP Stack / Meeting briefOP Stack · Product + Engineering

Transaction confirmation latency is hard to explain and improve.

Connect existing telemetry, clarify notifications, and remove avoidable waiting.

~60 msbest measured median

OP Sepolia: 67 ms p99 across 1,000 self-transfers. Client submission to first sub-block receipt, including network latency. Submissions were timed to sub-block production; signing, connection setup and deliberate pre-submission waiting were excluded.

01 Measurement / Establish a trustworthy baseline

Problem. Existing monitoring measures parts of the lifecycle, but we lack a verified, continuous baseline connecting those measurements to the confirmation experience. The benchmark does not isolate time spent inside the OP Stack.

Solution. Reuse ingress, builder and RPC instrumentation. Correlate first receipt at our RPC, ordering preconfirmation, and execution result availability through the serving RPC. Export Prometheus histograms; track median, p95/p99 and variance, with coverage, timeouts and dropped observations.

02 Developer experience / Make progress easy to consume

Problem. Applications need ordering confirmation, execution results, or both. Developers must reconcile RPC replies, receipts and sub-block streams, including what is provisional and what is final.

Solution. Provide consistent notifications without polling for either or both milestones. Include status and logs; support reverted transactions and transactions that emit no logs. Define revocations and recovery after reconnects. Milestones may arrive together.

03 Performance / Reduce waiting and variance

Problem. Delays and inconsistent confirmation times hurt time-sensitive applications. Improvements to individual components can miss the waiting between stages.

Solution. Use the baseline to pursue low-hanging fruit: eliminate mempool P2P from the submission-to-builder critical path through direct delivery, remove redundant handoffs, and reduce buffering and timer-driven waits. Validate each candidate and compare median, p95/p99 and variance under comparable workloads and submission timing.

Measurement boundary: “Result available” means the serving RPC exposes the execution result. The builder executes before publishing its ordering preconfirmation; getPayload is not a pre-execution milestone.

Evidence: OP Sepolia benchmark, 8 September 2026 (59.7 ms median; 67.1 ms p99). Existing monitor · Sub-block semantics
